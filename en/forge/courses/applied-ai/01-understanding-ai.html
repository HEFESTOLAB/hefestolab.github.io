Block 01 · Foundations

Understand before you use

A clear foundation for making decisions, asking better questions and verifying outputs.

Review priority: mediumReviewed: 20 AUG 2026
01Understand02Models03Context04Agents05MCP + local06The Forge

Block objective

By the end, someone with no prior AI training should be able to explain what a generative model does, distinguish what it knows from what it retrieves, recognise its limits and define a professional check before using an output.

  1. Generative artificial intelligence. Systems that produce new content — text, code, images or data — from learned patterns. They are not databases of exact answers.
  2. What an LLM is. A large language model estimates which pieces of information coherently follow the input. It may reason usefully, but it has no professional judgement or intent of its own.
  3. Tokens. Models process units that may be whole words, parts of words or punctuation. Limits and costs are generally measured in tokens, not pages.
  4. Context window. The available workspace for instructions, conversation, files and results. More context is not automatically better; it must be relevant and organised.
  5. Training versus inference. Training adjusts a model on large collections of data; inference uses the trained model to answer a specific input.
  6. Internal knowledge versus web search. A response may come from internal patterns or sources retrieved with a tool. Only the latter can bring in recent external information, and its sources still need checking.
  7. Hallucinations. A model can produce plausible but false data, invented references or unjustified confidence. Fluency is not evidence.
  8. Multimodality. Some systems work across text, images, audio, video or documents. Every modality adds capabilities and possible interpretation errors.
  9. Tools. Search, calculation, browsing, code and connectors extend what a model can do. The tool executes; the model decides how to request it within its permissions.
  10. Professional verification. Check assumptions, units, sources, standards, geometry and format before accepting an output. Final responsibility remains human.

Operating rule

If an answer affects safety, compliance, cost, measurements or a project decision, demand verifiable evidence and check it against the competent source.

Final practice

Dissect an answer

Ask an assistant to explain the difference between IFC and a BIM authoring model for a non-technical audience. Then:

  • separate conceptual claims from information that might change;
  • mark which sentences require a source;
  • identify ambiguous terms;
  • request a second version that clearly labels assumptions, limits and sources;
  • write a three-line verdict: usable, usable with corrections, or unusable.
