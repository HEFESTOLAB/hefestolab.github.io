An ecosystem, not a fixed league table

Catalogues, limits, plans and capabilities change. This block uses criteria that remain useful as model names evolve. Always check current documentation and provider terms.

  1. Current ecosystem. General, specialised, multimodal, reasoning and coding models coexist with open models inside assistants and platforms.
  2. ChatGPT and OpenAI. A conversational environment with files and tools whose exact feature set depends on the product, account and date.
  3. Claude and Anthropic. Model and product families for conversation and code; specific capabilities depend on the version and configuration available.
  4. Gemini and Google. Models and assistants connected to Google's ecosystem, with modalities and conditions that must be checked against official sources.
  5. Grok and other assistants. Alternatives that may be useful because of their integrations, access to information, modalities or user experience.
  6. Open models. Weights and licences with different levels of openness, hardware requirements and options for tuning or deployment.
  7. Cloud AI. Remote access to managed infrastructure makes powerful models accessible, while privacy, data residency and cost require review.
  8. Local AI. Running on your own hardware offers control and avoids sending content to the model provider, in exchange for local resources and operation.
  9. Privacy. Classify data before choosing: public, internal, confidential, contractual or regulated.
  10. Cost. Consider plan, usage, human time, infrastructure and repetition. A historical price is not a future guarantee.
  11. Speed. Latency, volume, queues, hardware and review time matter, not just generation speed.
  12. Context. Check how much the complete workflow can hold and whether documents, tools and answers compete for the same space.
  13. Task-based selection. Define the work first: writing, analysis, programming, search, interface operation or data processing.

A matrix for your next task
